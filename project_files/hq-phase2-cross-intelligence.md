## 0. Read this first — the challenge I'm obligated to make before designing further

Three tensions this directive doesn't resolve on its own. Naming them is the
same discipline `hq-v2-architecture.md` §0 already established — a design
that complies line-by-line without surfacing the conflicts underneath it
would be a worse document than one that stops and resolves them first.

**1. Phase I hasn't been load-bearing tested yet.** `hq-v2-architecture.md`
§13 lists nine implementation steps. Steps 1-4 are done. Steps 5-9 —
**a real Deployment run through `roster.md`, a real Debrief through the
gate, a Folder Dive/Recon pass fully promoted, a compaction pass on real
content, and only then a tooling decision** — have not happened. What has
run since is Reconnaissance (a real, valuable pass — 16 reports, real
findings) and two Opportunity Court board sessions. Neither of those is
the same as a mission actually deploying, being debriefed, and having its
lessons survive the gate. Designing a second floor — Cross Intelligence
Weights, a Training Division, a formal Promotion Board — on a foundation
that has never been tested under its own real weight is a real risk, not
a formality to wave past. Per the Company Thesis, engineering time is
capital; so is design time spent on a layer nothing has validated yet.

**My position:** design is cheap and durable — writing down what CIW *is*,
what belongs in it, and what governs it costs little and doesn't rot.
**Implementation is what waits.** This document is the design. I am
recommending against scaffolding any of it (no `HQ/CIW/` folder, no
`agent-training.md`, no Promotion Board convened) until `hq-v2-architecture.md`
§13 steps 5-8 have actually run at least once — the first real CIW
promotion candidate should be a genuine lesson from a genuine Debrief, not
a placeholder invented to prove the mechanism works. See §14 for where this
lands in a recommended order.

**2. CIW is not `briefing.md` renamed, and the two need a real dividing
line or this becomes duplication.** `briefing.md` (Tier 2, per
`hq-v2-architecture.md` §6) is a compact digest of **current state** —
regenerated by compaction, meant to go stale and be re-derived. CIW is
**durable judgment** — meant to survive the very state `briefing.md`
tracks. `strategic-direction.md` says of itself: "this file describes
where the company is right now, not a forecast." That line is exactly
the test for what can never enter CIW. §1 below draws this line
explicitly, item by item, because an ungoverned CIW that just re-absorbs
`briefing.md`'s current-state content is the bloat this whole initiative
exists to prevent.

**3. The directive lists "Promotion Board" as an evidence source
alongside Recon, Training, Debrief, and Founder decision — that's a
category error, and repeating it would build the wrong mechanism.**
Recon, Training, Debrief, Opportunity Court, and a Founder decision are
where evidence *comes from*. A Promotion Board is a *reviewing body* —
it consumes evidence, it doesn't generate it. `Recon/README.md` already
named this concept once, correctly, as a deferred consumer of "Internal
Recon" findings. §6 below treats Promotion Board as the review gate for
CIW specifically, not a sixth evidence source.

## 1. Cross Intelligence Weight (CIW) Architecture

### What CIW is

CIW is the company's **portable judgment layer** — the subset of
everything Headquarters knows that should survive a model swap, a
provider swap, a tool swap, or a full IDE/deployment change, because it
is judgment *about* the company, not a report *from* a moment in the
company's life. It is not a new knowledge base parallel to Company
Intelligence — it is Company Intelligence's most durable layer, distilled
and versioned as its own artifact.

### What belongs in CIW vs. what stays transient

This is the concrete answer to the directive's "determine which
information belongs inside CIW and which should remain transient,"
applied file-by-file against what already exists:

| Belongs in CIW (durable judgment) | Stays transient (Tier 1/2 only) |
|---|---|
| `company-thesis.md` — why the company exists, operating principles | `strategic-direction.md` — explicitly "now, not a forecast" |
| `founder-profile.md`'s durable strengths/constraints (stack, risk tolerance, time/audience constraints) | `founder-profile.md`'s situational notes (current employer status, in-flight corrections) |
| The Global Constitution (`_constitution.md`) — already *is* judgment, the seed CIW grows from | `products.md` — status changes constantly (active/iceboxed/rejected) |
| `coo`'s scoring rubric and Automatic Rejections (evidence-over-hype, distribution-first) | `project-history.md`, `Recon/*` reports — point-in-time findings |
| `cto`'s Feasibility Doctrine (stack-fit first, reuse verified not assumed, cheapest experiment first) | Deployment memories — mission-local by design, never durable by default |
| Curated, cross-mission-confirmed entries from `patterns.md` (a pattern seen 2+ missions running, not a single observation) | A single mission's raw Debrief answers before promotion |
| Capability Graph's **maturity/confidence judgments** (§3) — "this is a proven strength" as a standing assessment | Capability Graph's **activity log** — which mission last touched a capability |

The test, restated as one line: **if it would still be true after every
current project in the portfolio shipped, failed, or was abandoned, it
belongs in CIW. If it describes what's happening now, it doesn't.**

### Required documents (CIW/ contents)

```
HQ/CIW/
├── version.md              # current version number, date, cut criteria met, size
├── CHANGELOG.md             # dated version-bump log — what changed, which evidence triggered it
├── thesis.md                 # distilled from company-thesis.md — why the company exists, principles
├── founder-standing.md       # durable founder profile: strengths, constraints, risk posture — not situational status
├── standards.md               # the Global Constitution's rules, restated as the CIW's own copy of record
├── decision-rubrics.md        # coo's scoring weights + automatic rejections, cto's feasibility doctrine, cfo's/security's standing thresholds — the actual judgment functions executives apply
├── capability-judgments.md    # maturity/confidence/reuse-potential per capability — cross-references Capability Graph (§3), doesn't duplicate its structure
└── lessons.md                  # cross-mission-confirmed durable lessons only — a small, curated file, not patterns.md copied over
```

Each file keeps the header convention already established for executives
(`hq-v2-architecture.md` §8) instead of inventing a second metadata
format: a `# CIW: {name}` / `# Distilled from: {Tier 1 source}` /
`# Version: {N}` / `# Evidence count: {N}` header. One convention, reused.

### Structure — why plain markdown, not embeddings or a fine-tune

This is the decision the whole vendor-agnostic requirement rests on.
Two tempting alternatives, both rejected on purpose:

- **Fine-tuning a model on company history** locks the judgment to one
  model family and one provider's training pipeline — the opposite of
  "survives a model change." A fine-tune is not portable; it's the
  single most vendor-*coupled* thing this initiative could produce.
- **Embeddings / a vector store** are provider- and schema-specific
  (dimension count, distance metric, index format) and represent
  *retrieval convenience*, not judgment — they'd need to be regenerated
  per embedding model anyway, which is the maintenance burden this
  design exists to avoid.

Plain, versioned markdown, read directly into context, is the only
format that loads identically regardless of which frontier model is
doing the reading. This is the same bet `hq-v2-architecture.md` already
made (markdown+git over a Tauri app) — CIW just makes it explicit as
the load-bearing design principle for institutional judgment
specifically, not just for filing convenience.

### Versioning strategy

CIW versions as **whole, immutable snapshots**, not per-file edits —
because judgment is evaluated as an integrated set (does this new
belief contradict that standing one), not as isolated facts:

- Git is the versioning substrate — a CIW version bump is a commit
  tagged `ciw-v{N}`, exactly like the repo already versions everything
  else. No parallel versioning mechanism gets invented.
- `version.md` records the current version number, the date it was cut,
  which evidence (which Debrief/Training/Recon/Opportunity-Court/Founder
  decision) triggered it, and a size figure checked against the
  token-efficiency goal each time.
- `CHANGELOG.md` is the append-only, human-readable history of version
  bumps — one entry per version, never rewritten, same convention as
  `HQ/DECISIONS.md` already uses for structural decisions.
- Previous CIW versions are **not duplicated on disk** — retrievable via
  `git show ciw-v{N-1}` the same way any other historical file state is,
  consistent with `_changelog/{agent}.md`'s existing "one entry per
  version bump," not a full-copy history.
- A version bumps only at a defined cadence tied to the Learning Loop
  (§7) — after a Training cycle or a Debrief's `[HQ-CANDIDATE]` item
  clears the Promotion Board (§6) — never continuously, and never for a
  single unconfirmed observation.

### Update process and promotion workflow

Covered in full in §6 (Knowledge Governance) and §7 (Learning Loop) —
summarized here: evidence lands in Tier 1 Company Intelligence first
(existing gate, unchanged), proves durable across more than one
occurrence or an explicit Founder decision, then goes through Librarian
pre-screen → Promotion Board review → Founder sign-off → CIW version
bump. No executive writes CIW directly, ever — stronger than the
existing Tier 1 gate, because CIW changes the company's portable
identity, not just one file's content.

### Snapshot strategy

A CIW snapshot is the full `HQ/CIW/` directory at a tagged commit. This
is what "any frontier model loaded in Year 10" actually loads — not the
whole repo, not `briefing.md`'s current-state digest, just this
directory at its current version. Portable in the literal sense: it
could be copied out of this repo entirely and still make sense on its
own, which is the test a good CIW snapshot should pass and `briefing.md`
was never designed to.

### Loading strategy and token optimization

Extends the existing three-tier escalation
(`hq-v2-architecture.md` §6) with CIW inserted as the new stable base:

```
INDEX.md (nav, cheapest)
  → CIW/ (durable judgment baseline — loaded for nearly every dispatch)
    → briefing.md (current-state digest — loaded when "what's happening now" matters)
      → Tier 1 source file (full detail — rare)
```

The token-optimization payoff is specifically CIW's **stability**: unlike
`briefing.md`, which is recompacted on a cadence and therefore changes
often, a CIW version is immutable between promotions — the same content,
byte-for-byte, across every dispatch in that window. That's exactly the
shape prompt caching rewards (this very session's environment runs on a
cache with a real TTL) — CIW being boring and unchanging between
promotions is a feature, not a limitation. `briefing.md` stays the
volatile, frequently-recompacted layer; CIW is the one that's cheap
precisely because it doesn't move.

**This only holds if the two layers don't overlap.** `briefing.md` as it
exists today opens with "The founder" and "The thesis" sections —
content that, under this design, now belongs in `CIW/founder-standing.md`
and `CIW/thesis.md`. Loading both unmodified would mean paying for the
same judgment twice on every dispatch, which directly undercuts the
caching argument above. **Required rule, not optional:** once CIW exists,
Librarian's Compaction Pass must exclude anything the current CIW version
already owns — `briefing.md` becomes current-state-only ("where the
company actually is," open questions, in-flight corrections), CIW owns
durable judgment, and the two are disjoint by construction. This is a
gated follow-on edit to `agent-librarian.md`'s Compaction Pass
responsibility, same flavor as the `coo`/`research` follow-ons already
flagged in §2 and §4 — not made silently in this document.

## 2. Training Division Architecture

### What Training is, and isn't

Training is not a project and not a mission — it produces no shipped
deliverable and consumes no Deployment. Its output is: recommendations,
executive discussion, company policy proposals, capability improvements,
and CIW update proposals. It exists so the company gets measurably
better at reasoning over time, independent of any specific opportunity.

### No new agent — Training is an operation, the same pattern Recon already set

`hq-v2-architecture.md` already resolved this exact question for Recon:
reuse assessment was already `cto`'s Core Responsibility, so Recon didn't
need an 8th board seat — it needed a process. Training follows the same
discipline. Rather than hiring an 18th executive, Training is a
**recurring operation**, coordinated by `research` (whose existing charter
— "market researcher, trend analyst" — is the closest fit to "ingest an
external source and synthesize it") with the domain-owning executive
pulled in per source type:

| Source type | Domain lead |
|---|---|
| Framework/API documentation, technical papers | `cto` |
| Security standards, engineering standards | `security` |
| Industry reports, competitive research | `coo`/`marketing` |
| Business literature | `cfo`/`product` |
| Books (general) | `research`, coordinating whichever domain lead the content actually touches |

`research`'s own agent definition would need a scope-extension proposal
(from "market/competitor intel" to "external-knowledge ingestion
generally") — flagged here as a required follow-on change, not made
silently in this document; agent-definition edits are gated the same way
any HQ write is.

### Process (mirrors Recon's shape exactly)

1. `research` (or the domain lead, for a source clearly inside one
   domain) reads the source and extracts findings.
2. Output: `Training/{source-slug}-{date}.md`, same discipline as
   `Recon/README.md`'s template — a **standardized report**, not raw
   notes:

```markdown
# Training: {source-slug} — {date}
## Source
(title, author/org, why this source was selected)
## Findings relevant to current capabilities
## Findings relevant to current risks/gaps
## Recommended capability improvements
## Recommended policy proposals
## Recommended CIW update candidates
(flagged, not promoted — same discipline as Debrief's [HQ-CANDIDATE])
## Executive discussion required
(which executives should weigh in before this is filed, and why)
```

3. Librarian pre-screens exactly as it does for Recon/Debrief — dedup,
   conflict-check, phrasing check.
4. Genuine cross-domain findings get an actual Board Convening session
   (reusing `agent-ceo.md`'s existing pattern) rather than a single
   agent's unilateral call — Training findings that touch multiple
   domains deserve the same "no rubber-stamping" discipline Opportunity
   Court already applies to its own inputs.
5. CEO/Founder approves filing into Tier 1 Company Intelligence.
   CIW-level promotion requires the full gate in §6, same as any other
   evidence source.

### Governance

Training never writes Company Intelligence or CIW directly — same two
gates as everything else (Librarian pre-screen, then approval).
Cadence is deliberately not fixed to "every N days" — Training work
competes for the founder's actual engineering-hours-as-capital, so it
runs when there's a specific, named source worth the time, not on a
forced schedule that would generate busywork to fill a calendar slot.

## 3. Capability Graph Specification

### Why `capabilities.md` (flat table) isn't sufficient

The current file is a role → domain lookup table — useful, but it can't
represent what the directive actually asks for: hierarchy, dependency,
market adjacency, or growth opportunity between capabilities. Those are
graph relationships, not table rows.

### Structure — still plain markdown, still one file at current scale

Given ~15-20 known capabilities today, a single structured file
(`HQ/CompanyIntelligence/capability-graph.md`) is sufficient — escalating
to one-file-per-capability only if the graph grows past a size where a
single file stops being scannable, the same "don't over-engineer until
the scale demands it" discipline `briefing.md`'s compaction already
follows. `capabilities.md` is superseded by this file, not kept as a
parallel document — migrating its current content is a follow-on
implementation step, not done in this design pass.

Each node:

```markdown
### {capability-name}
- **Hierarchy:** parent capability (if any) — e.g. "AI Orchestration" is a
  child of "Software Engineering," a sibling of "Workflow Automation"
- **Depends on:** [other capability nodes required for this one to function]
- **Enables:** [other capability nodes or product categories this unlocks]
- **Market adjacency:** what commercial category this capability is
  recognizable in (e.g. OCR → document-intelligence SaaS)
- **Maturity:** Proven-in-production / Demonstrated / Theoretical
- **Confidence:** per the Global Constitution's confidence standard —
  High only if verified against real shipped code, not inferred from a
  stack description
- **Reuse potential:** which known projects could draw on this today
- **Growth opportunity:** the specific next capability this could extend
  into, if any — not a generic "could be useful"
```

### How Recon updates the graph

Recon's "technical capabilities found" section (existing template,
§4 below) is the single input path. A capability node is added or
strengthened only when Recon (any mode, §4) verifies it against real
code or a real completed engagement — never speculatively declared. The
same Librarian gate applies: Recon proposes a node/edge change, never
writes the graph directly.

### What promotes from Capability Graph into CIW

Only the **maturity/confidence judgment itself** (§1's table) — "this is
a proven strength, verified across N projects" — not the graph's
activity log or its full dependency structure, which stays current-state
detail in Tier 1.

## 4. Recon v2 Specification

### Recon becomes the single discovery umbrella — this is what resolves "Opportunity Court no longer discovers"

The directive's cleanest resolution: rather than inventing a parallel
discovery mechanism, **every kind of discovery becomes a Recon mode**,
each led by the domain-appropriate executive, each producing the same
standardized report shape, each gated identically by Librarian. This
makes "discovery belongs to Recon" literally true instead of aspirational:

| Mode | Leads | Discovers |
|---|---|---|
| **External Recon** (existing) | `cto` | Internal repo assets — engines, components, patterns, technical debt |
| **Market Recon** (new — absorbs `coo`'s current live-search Core Responsibility) | `coo` | External demand signals — marketplaces, forums, issue trackers, competitor research |
| **Internal Recon** (existing, still deferred) | Librarian, feeding Promotion Board | Executive performance across missions |

This requires a follow-on, gated change to `agent-coo.md`'s Core
Responsibilities (move "Search" from a live, in-session action to a
pre-produced Market Recon report `coo` reads at Opportunity Court time)
and to `agent-ceo.md`'s Board Convening order (coo's board seat becomes
"present pre-gathered Market Recon findings," not "search live") — named
here as required, not made silently in this document.

### Standardized report — extends the existing template with the directive's full list

`Recon/README.md`'s six-section template already covers reusable
engines/components/capabilities/patterns/unfinished work/abandoned
opportunities. The directive's fuller list maps on cleanly as additions,
not a rewrite:

```markdown
# Recon: {mode} / {target-slug} — {date}

## Capabilities found          (existing: "technical capabilities found")
## Assets found                (NEW — technical + IP + relationship assets, not just code)
## Products found               (existing, under "abandoned opportunities" — now its own section)
## Reusable engines found       (existing)
## Hidden assets found          (NEW — the "arguably the strongest opportunity in company history"
                                  class of finding, per xpanda-ops-platform precedent)
## Technical debt found         (NEW — explicit, not folded into "unfinished work")
## Market adjacencies found     (NEW — for Market Recon mode specifically)
## Opportunity seeds found      (NEW — under-evidenced signals worth a future look, not yet a scored opportunity)
## Strategic leverage found     (NEW — where this finding changes the company's negotiating/build position)
## Knowledge gaps found         (NEW — what this pass could NOT determine, and why)
## Surprising findings          (NEW — explicit, since these are exactly what's easiest to bury in a routine report)
## Assumption changes           (NEW — mirrors what already happened informally with xpanda-ops-platform's
                                  ownership correction; this makes it a named, expected report section)
## Business risks found         (NEW)
## Capability growth found      (NEW — feeds Capability Graph §3 directly)

## Recommended Company Intelligence updates
## Recommended Capability Graph updates
## Recommended CIW update candidates
(flagged only — same discipline as Training's report, never a direct write)
```

Same gate as before: Librarian pre-screens, CEO/Founder approves before
anything lands.

## 5. Opportunity Court V2 — Inputs

Opportunity Court stops gathering evidence mid-session and becomes purely
an evaluation board. Its board-convening mechanic in `agent-ceo.md`
doesn't change; what changes is what each seat reads *before* reasoning,
and that `coo`'s seat no longer performs a live search (§4):

| Input | Source | Replaces (in the old flow) |
|---|---|---|
| Cross Intelligence Weights | `HQ/CIW/` (current version) | Nothing — this is new, the durable-judgment baseline every seat now reasons from |
| Company Intelligence | `briefing.md` + relevant Tier 1 files | Same as today |
| Capability Graph | `capability-graph.md` | Today's flat `capabilities.md` |
| Founder Profile | `founder-profile.md` (situational) + `CIW/founder-standing.md` (durable) | Same, now split by durability |
| Company Thesis | `CIW/thesis.md` | `company-thesis.md` directly |
| Recon Reports | `Recon/*` — all three modes, pre-produced | `coo`'s live search, done in-session |
| Training Outcomes | `Training/*` | Nothing — new input |
| Current Constraints | `strategic-direction.md`'s open-question section | Same as today |
| Mission Objectives | Whatever the founder's ask actually is this session | Same as today |

The board still reasons independently, still disagrees openly, still
resolves by the CEO reasoning through conflict rather than averaging —
none of `agent-ceo.md`'s Board Convening mechanic changes, only what's
loaded going in.

## 6. Knowledge Governance Framework

**No executive may directly modify CIW.** This is a stronger bar than the
existing Tier 1 gate (Librarian pre-screen → CEO/user approval), because
CIW is the artifact meant to define the company's identity to a model
that has never seen this repo before — a wrong entry here is far more
expensive to leave uncaught than a wrong entry in a single Tier 1 file.

### Evidence sources (generate candidates — never approve them)

Recon (any mode), Training, Debrief, Opportunity Court, Founder decision.

### Promotion Board (reviews candidates — generates nothing itself)

Corrects the directive's category error (§0.3): the Promotion Board is
the review gate, not a source. It is a **board-convening session**,
reusing the exact mechanic already proven in Opportunity Court — no new
agent, no new convening pattern, just a different reading list and a
different question ("does this belong in the company's portable
identity," not "is this opportunity worth pursuing").

### Approval workflow

```
Evidence (Recon / Training / Debrief / Opportunity Court / Founder decision)
   ↓
Lands in Tier 1 Company Intelligence first — existing gate, unchanged
   ↓
Proves durable: confirmed across 2+ independent occurrences,
OR is an explicit, one-time Founder decision (the standing already
established for e.g. the Obsidian-retirement / ERP-firewall-exception
entries in HQ/DECISIONS.md — a founder decision doesn't need repetition
to be durable)
   ↓
Librarian pre-screens against the CIW-worthiness test (§1's table) —
duplicate? contradicts standing CIW? phrased as a durable rule, not a
one-off war story?
   ↓
Promotion Board reviews — does this integrate cleanly with the current
CIW version, or does it contradict something standing that needs
reconciling first?
   ↓
Founder sign-off — explicit, higher bar than routine Tier 1 promotion
   ↓
Librarian bumps CIW version (git tag), writes CHANGELOG.md entry,
regenerates INDEX.md
```

This is the same shape as the existing Debrief/Recon gate
(`hq-v2-architecture.md` §5, §7) with one additional review layer
(Promotion Board) and one stricter final approver (Founder specifically,
not "CEO or the user" — CIW changes warrant the highest bar in the
system).

## 7. Company Learning Loop — governance at every stage

```
Training           → research/domain-lead operate it; Librarian gate; CEO/Founder file to Tier 1
  ↓
Recon               → cto/coo/Librarian (3 modes); Librarian gate; CEO/Founder file to Tier 1
  ↓
Company Intelligence → Tier 1 is the landing zone for both — existing gate, unchanged
  ↓
Cross Intelligence Weights → Promotion Board reviews Tier 1 entries that have proven durable;
                               Founder signs off; version bumps
  ↓
Opportunity Court   → reads CIW + Company Intelligence + Capability Graph + Founder/Thesis +
                       Recon + Training outputs; evaluates only, discovers nothing; reasons
                       independently per agent-ceo.md's existing Board Convening mechanic
  ↓
Mission              → Founder go/no-go (existing standard, hq-v2-architecture.md §9);
                        CEO scaffolds Deployment (roster.md + brief.md, existing §3-§4)
  ↓
Deployment           → executives operate per pinned HQ version + mission overlay + local memory
                        (existing, unchanged)
  ↓
Debrief               → the eight-question format (existing §5), [HQ-CANDIDATE]/[HEADLINE] flagged
  ↓
Promotion             → Librarian pre-screen → CEO/user (Tier 1) or Promotion Board/Founder (CIW) —
                         two altitudes of the same gate mechanic, different stakes
  ↓
Training (loop closes) → Debrief's flagged Knowledge Gaps and Training's own "executive
                          discussion required" items become the next cycle's source-selection input
```

Nothing in this loop is a new mechanism end to end — it's the existing
Debrief/Recon gate, run twice (once for Tier 1, once — rarer, stricter —
for CIW), with Training added as a second evidence-producing operation
alongside Recon.

## 9. CIW Versioning Strategy (summary — full detail in §1)

Whole-snapshot, git-tag versioning (`ciw-v{N}`), bumped only at a
Promotion Board-cleared cadence, `CHANGELOG.md` as the append-only record,
no duplicated historical copies on disk, `version.md` as the current
pointer. Directly reuses the versioning discipline `hq-v2-architecture.md`
§8 already established for executive files — no second versioning
convention invented.

  correcting).
- **A read-only CIW/Capability Graph viewer** — same deferred-until-proven
  standard as `hq-v2-architecture.md` §14's Company Intelligence viewer;
  worth building only once several real CIW version bumps exist to view.
- **A second CIW tier for market/industry judgment**, separate from
  company-internal judgment, if the portfolio ever spans distinct enough
  markets that one shared `decision-rubrics.md` stops fitting all of them
  — explicitly not needed at current single-founder, single-portfolio
  scale; flagged so it isn't rediscovered as a surprise later.

## 11. Final Question — Answered

**How should Cross Intelligence Weights evolve so that any frontier AI
model loaded in Year 10 immediately operates with the company's
accumulated judgment, standards, philosophy, and strategic reasoning?**

By never being made of anything a model can do *for* you instead of
*with* you. CIW works in Year 10 for the same reason it has to work
today: it is plain, versioned text — no embeddings tied to one provider's
vector format, no fine-tune tied to one model family, no proprietary tool
config. A frontier model in Year 10 loads `HQ/CIW/` at its current
version and gets, in the first few thousand tokens: why the company
exists (`thesis.md`), who it's actually optimizing for and what that
founder will and won't do (`founder-standing.md`), the rules every
executive has always operated under (`standards.md`), the actual scoring
and feasibility functions the company applies to a new opportunity
(`decision-rubrics.md`), a judgment about what the company is genuinely
strong at after ten years of evidence (`capability-judgments.md`), and
the handful of lessons that proved true across more than one mission
(`lessons.md`) — each one there because it survived a Promotion Board and
a Founder's explicit sign-off, not because a model once said something
plausible-sounding about it.

The reasoning engine changes every year or two. The Global Constitution's
Rule 8 — no rubber-stamping, evidence changes decisions — doesn't. That
asymmetry is the entire bet this design makes: judgment that's cheap to
version, easy to audit, and impossible to lock to one vendor outlives
every model that ever reasoned over it.
