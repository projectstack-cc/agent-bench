# Recon: Horizontal business-function overhead — 2026-07-20

**Type:** External market research (`coo`-led), third in the founder-
directed cross-market opportunity-scan series (see
`strategic-direction.md`), following the PC gaming and e-commerce scans
run earlier this same session.

**Scope, per the founder's direction:** step outside specific industries
and scan named business functions directly — Sales, Customer support,
Procurement, HR, Finance, Inventory, Scheduling, Compliance, Training,
Document management, Reporting, Quality management, Asset management.

**Ledger check performed first:** two of the thirteen — Compliance and
Quality management — already have a real prior finding from the
2026-07-17 domain-experience research pass: SafetyCulture dominates both
OSHA/safety compliance and QC/inspection checklists (`products.md`). Not
re-researched from scratch; factored in directly.

---

## Findings, by function

Ten functions were freshly searched (Sales, Customer support, Procurement,
HR, Finance/AP-AR, Scheduling, Training, Document management, Asset
management, Inventory), with Compliance and Quality management carried
forward from the existing ledger finding. **Every one of the thirteen
shows the identical shape:** a mature, named SaaS category, free templates
freely available at the bottom, and paid competitors at every price tier
up to enterprise:

| Function | What's already there |
|---|---|
| Sales | Full CRM category (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive) down to free spreadsheet templates |
| Customer support | Zendesk plus a named alternatives tier (Intercom, Freshdesk, Help Scout, Tidio, Ringly) |
| Procurement | ERP procurement modules, accounting-software PO generation (QuickBooks), spreadsheet templates for the smallest tier |
| HR | Full HRIS category; spreadsheets as the acknowledged budget fallback |
| Finance (AP/AR) | Accounting software (QuickBooks) plus a template ecosystem specifically for the smallest businesses |
| Scheduling | Homebase and a named template ecosystem, explicitly positioned by team size |
| Training/certification tracking | TalentGuard and others explicitly selling against "spreadsheets shut down your business" |
| Document management | SuiteFiles, Axis, and others, explicit ROI framing (up to 240 hrs/year claimed savings) |
| Asset/maintenance management | Tractian and a template ecosystem, explicitly organized by industry (forklifts, HVAC, etc.) |
| Inventory (general, non-ecommerce) | POS-integrated inventory (Toast, Restroworks) plus templates, by vertical (restaurant, retail) |
| Reporting | No sharp findings either way — too broad/generic a query to surface real complaint evidence, not a positive finding |
| Compliance | Known: SafetyCulture dominant (2026-07-17 finding) |
| Quality management | Known: SafetyCulture dominant (2026-07-17 finding) |

None of the ten fresh searches surfaced a specific, differentiated,
underserved gap — every function returned an established category leader
plus a template-tier fallback, the same two-ended saturation shape found
in both prior scans.

---

## The actual finding of this pass — a strategic one, not a product one

**Named horizontal business functions are, if anything, more contested
than either vertical market scanned so far.** PC gaming and e-commerce are
each one industry; "Sales," "HR," "Scheduling," etc. are each a
cross-industry category — the single most obvious unit of market
segmentation in all of software, targeted continuously since the 1990s
ERP wave through today's indie/microSaaS wave. Enumerating them by name
and searching for pain is, structurally, searching in the most heavily
mapped territory that exists.

**This is a different mechanism than the app-store-distribution pattern**
found in the first two scans (`patterns.md`) — it's not about
marketplace discoverability, it's that a *named* function is inherently
guessable by every other founder who ever asked "what costs businesses
money." The two patterns compound: a named function *with* strong
app-store-style distribution (e.g., "scheduling" + Shopify App Store) is
close to the most contested territory searchable at all.

**Implication, stated directly rather than softened:** searching "which
named function/named industry has pain" a fourth time, the same way, is
likely to produce a fourth rejection for the same structural reason. The
axis not yet tried is the **intersection** — a specific function, for a
specific narrow vertical or persona, where general tools are known to be
a poor fit (too broad, wrong workflow shape, priced for a bigger business)
— not a general function searched across all industries at once. This is
consistent with the company's own domain-experience research precedent
(2026-07-17): the four hypotheses that came from real, specific,
lived-in operational context all found real pain, even though none
cleared the crowding bar. Specificity found real pain reliably; genuine
open lanes require a specificity level this pass didn't use.

---

## Overall verdict

**Researched and rejected — all thirteen functions, in their general
form.** Filed in `products.md` so the same list isn't re-scanned at this
level of generality again. **Recommendation carried to
`strategic-direction.md`:** the next scan should target a specific
function-×-vertical or function-×-persona intersection, not another
general function or industry taken alone.

**Confidence:** High on the specific competitive-landscape claims cited
per function. Medium-High on the "search the intersection, not the
axis alone" recommendation — it's a reasoned inference from three
consecutive results forming the same shape, not independently verified
by having actually tried the intersection approach yet.
